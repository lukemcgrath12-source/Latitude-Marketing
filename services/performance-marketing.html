Performance Marketing

Measured By Enquiries, Not Impressions

Reach, impressions, and engagement are easy numbers to report and easy numbers to hide behind. The figures that matter to a small business are how many people got in touch, what those jobs were worth, and what it cost to get them. This is the layer that connects every channel to those three numbers.

The Problem

Why Most Small Business Reporting Is Useless

If you've had an agency before and couldn't tell whether it was working, this is usually why.

  • Reports full of reach and impressions, which tell you people saw something and nothing about whether it worked.
  • No conversion tracking, so attributing an enquiry to a channel is guesswork dressed up as data.
  • Phone calls not tracked at all, which for most trades is where the majority of enquiries actually land.
  • Each channel reported separately, so nobody ever sees whether they're helping each other or competing.
  • A monthly PDF nobody reads, full of charts that don't lead to a decision.
  • No agreed definition of what a good month looks like, so the goalposts move every time.

The Work

What's Involved

This sits above the individual channels. It's the measurement, the plan, and the decision making that keeps everything pointed at enquiries.

01

Measurement Setup

Analytics, conversion events, form tracking, and call tracking configured properly. Until this exists, every other decision is an opinion.

02

Channel Plan

Deciding where the budget goes and in what order. For most local businesses that's search first, then local, then social, but it depends entirely on how people find your kind of business.

03

Conversion Work

Improving what happens after the click. Page structure, enquiry paths, response times. Lifting conversion rate is usually cheaper than buying more traffic.

04

Campaign Management

Running the activity across channels as one campaign rather than separate projects, so the message stays consistent from ad to landing page to follow up.

05

Monthly Reporting

Written in plain English. What we spent, what came in, what it cost per enquiry, what we're changing next month, and why. Short enough that you'll actually read it.

06

Ongoing Optimisation

Moving budget toward what's producing enquiries and away from what isn't. That includes telling you when something we recommended isn't working.

This is for businesses running several things at once and needing someone to decide where the next dollar goes. Website, content, local search, and paid all measured together against a single set of numbers.

Questions

Straight Answers

What do you actually count as a result?

Enquiries. Form submissions, phone calls, and messages. We agree what counts before anything starts so there's no argument later about whether a month was good.

Can you track phone calls?

Yes. For most trades and service businesses the phone is where the majority of enquiries land, and leaving it untracked means you're measuring the smaller half of your results.

What if the numbers say something isn't working?

Then we say so and change it. Reporting that only ever contains good news isn't reporting.

Is this only for businesses spending a lot?

No, but there's a floor. If you're running one channel on a small budget, you probably want that channel managed well rather than a measurement layer on top of it. We'll tell you which situation you're in.

How often do we speak?

Monthly reporting as a minimum, and you can reach Luke directly in between. There's no ticket queue to go through.
