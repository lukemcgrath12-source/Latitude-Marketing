Brand Strategy

Get The Message Right Before You Pay For Reach

Brand isn't a logo. It's what someone thinks when your name comes up, and whether they can tell inside five seconds what you do, who you do it for, and why you rather than the other three quotes sitting on their kitchen bench.

The Problem

What Unclear Positioning Costs You

Positioning problems don't announce themselves. They show up as everything else being harder and more expensive than it should be.

  • Ads cost more, because unclear messages get scrolled past and you pay for the attention either way.
  • Content is a struggle to write, because nobody has decided what the business actually stands for.
  • Quotes turn into price comparisons, because the customer can't see a difference between you and the next quote.
  • You're chasing every type of job, which means you're never the obvious choice for any of them.
  • Your website, your van, your invoices, and your Instagram all look like different businesses.
  • You explain what you do differently every time somebody asks.

The Work

What's Involved

Usually done before a website build or a first ad campaign, so the money that follows is spent on a message that holds up.

01

Positioning

What you do, who it's for, and the reason someone chooses you over the alternative. Written down in one paragraph that everything else has to agree with.

02

Audience Definition

Who's actually worth chasing. Which jobs are profitable, which are a headache, and where the customers you want are already spending their attention.

03

Messaging Framework

Your core message, the proof points behind it, and the way you answer the questions that come up on every quote. Plain language, ready to use on the phone as much as on the site.

04

Visual Identity

Logo, colours, typography, and how they get used. Built so it works on a phone screen, a shirt, a van, and a printed invoice without falling apart.

05

Brand Guidelines

A short, usable document that means anyone touching your marketing later produces something that looks like you. Files supplied in the formats you'll actually need.

06

Rollout

Applied across the website, social channels, ads, and anything else customer facing, so the whole thing lands at once rather than in fragments.

A Useful Distinction

Identity Is Not Strategy

Plenty of businesses buy the first and skip the second, then wonder why a nice looking logo didn't change anything.

Visual Identity Is What It Looks Like

Logo, colours, type, and the system that holds them together. Genuinely important. It's what makes a one person operation look like a business worth trusting with a twenty thousand dollar job. On its own though, it doesn't tell anyone why to choose you.

Brand Strategy Is What It Means

The decision about who you're for and what you're better at. It's what makes the copy write itself, gives the ads an angle, and stops every conversation defaulting to price. Do this first and the visual work has something to express.

Questions

Straight Answers

Do I need this, or do I just need a logo?

If you already know exactly who you're for and why people pick you, and you can say it the same way twice, you probably just need the visual work. If you found that sentence hard to finish, the strategy is the part that's missing.

Can this be done alongside a website build?

Yes, and it usually is. Doing it first is better, because the site then gets built around a decided message instead of the message being reverse engineered out of a finished site.

Will you change my business name?

Only if there's a real reason and you want to. Changing a name that already has recognition and reviews attached to it is a serious decision, and most of the time the answer is no.

What do I actually get at the end?

A positioning statement, an audience definition, a messaging framework you can use in conversation, the visual identity files, and a brand guidelines document. All yours to keep and use with anyone.

Is this worth it for a small operation?

It's worth more for a small operation than a large one, because you have less budget to waste on a message that doesn't land. That said, if your immediate problem is that nobody can find you at all, we'd point you at Google Business Profile and your website first.
