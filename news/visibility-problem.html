Google Ads for Tradies: What Works, What Doesn't, and What It Costs

Google Ads can generate consistent leads for trade businesses — but only if the campaign is set up correctly.

Google Ads is one of the most powerful lead generation tools available to trade businesses in Sydney. When someone types "emergency plumber Sutherland Shire" or "electrician near me" into Google, they are actively looking to hire someone right now. Showing up at that moment is enormously valuable. But a badly set-up Google Ads campaign will drain your budget with nothing to show for it. Here's what you need to know before you spend a dollar.

Why Google Ads works well for tradies

Unlike social media advertising — where you're showing your business to people who may or may not need you — Google Search Ads target people with active, immediate intent. They've typed in a search term. They need someone. They're ready to call. For trade services like plumbing, electrical, building, landscaping, and cleaning, this is the highest-quality traffic you can buy. The conversion rate from a well-targeted Google search ad is significantly higher than almost any other paid channel.

What a good Google Ads campaign looks like

A well-structured campaign for a tradie has three core elements. First, tightly targeted keywords — specific service and location combinations like "blocked drain plumber Miranda" rather than broad terms like "plumber." Broad terms attract too many irrelevant clicks and burn budget fast. Second, compelling ad copy that leads with what the searcher needs — availability, speed, guaranteed work, free quotes. Third, a landing page that matches the ad — if someone clicks an ad for emergency electrical work, they should land on a page specifically about emergency electrical, not your general homepage.

"The search intent on Google is unmatched. Someone typing 'plumber Cronulla' right now wants to hire someone today. That's your ad."

What doesn't work — and why budgets get wasted

The most common mistakes we see are broad keyword targeting, sending ad traffic to a homepage that doesn't convert, and running campaigns without negative keywords — which means your ad shows up for irrelevant searches like "plumber salary" or "how to fix a tap yourself" and you pay for every click. Google Ads run without proper setup and ongoing management will almost always underperform. The platform is powerful but it rewards expertise and attention.

What does it actually cost?

For trade services in Sydney, cost-per-click typically ranges from $8 to $35 depending on the trade and the competitiveness of the suburb. A realistic starting budget is $1,500 to $2,500 per month, which should generate enough clicks to produce meaningful data and a steady flow of leads. The key metric to watch is cost-per-lead — not cost-per-click. A $20 click that converts into a $2,000 job is excellent value. The goal is always return on ad spend, not just traffic volume.

Before you run ads, check your foundation

Google Ads will send traffic to your website. If your website is slow, hard to navigate on mobile, or doesn't have a clear call to action, you'll pay for clicks that don't convert. Before investing in paid search, make sure your website loads fast, looks professional on mobile, and has your phone number and a contact form prominently on every page. The ads drive the traffic. The website converts it.

Is Google Ads right for your business right now?

Google Ads is the right move when you have a solid website, you're in a service category where people actively search on Google, and you have a monthly budget of at least $1,500 to invest consistently. If you're not sure whether your website and Google presence are ready for paid traffic, a free visibility audit will tell you exactly where you stand and what to fix first.

Why Your Instagram Isn't Getting Enquiries (And What To Do)

Posting regularly but hearing nothing back? The problem isn't your content — it's your strategy.

You're posting. You're showing up. You're putting time into photos, captions, even the odd Reel — and almost nothing is coming back. No DMs, no link clicks, no calls. If this sounds familiar, you're not alone. It's one of the most common frustrations we hear from Sydney business owners, and the fix is almost never what people expect.

Instagram doesn't owe you enquiries

The first thing to understand is that Instagram is a discovery platform, not a leads platform — at least not by default. People come to Instagram to scroll, to be entertained, to get inspired. They are not in "buying mode" the way someone searching Google is. That doesn't mean Instagram can't generate enquiries. It absolutely can. But it requires a deliberate strategy, not just a posting schedule.

The three reasons your posts aren't converting

Most Instagram accounts that aren't generating enquiries have the same three problems. First, the content isn't speaking to the right person — it's too generic, too polished, or too focused on aesthetics rather than relevance. Second, there's no clear next step — posts end without telling the viewer what to do or why they should do it. Third, the profile itself doesn't convert — the bio is vague, there's no link to a booking page or contact form, and the highlights don't build trust.

"Posting is not a strategy. Posting with intention, a clear audience, and a path to enquiry — that's a strategy."

What content actually drives enquiries

The content that consistently drives enquiries shares three qualities. It speaks directly to a specific problem your ideal customer has. It shows your work in context — not just a finished result, but the before, the process, and the outcome. And it ends with a reason to act now — a limited offer, a specific call to action, or a low-friction next step like a free consultation. Mix this with behind-the-scenes content and customer stories and you have a content strategy that builds trust and drives action.

Fix your profile before you fix your posts

Before you change a single post, audit your profile. Your bio should say who you help, what you do, and where you're based — in two lines or less. Your link should go directly to a page that makes it easy to get in touch or book something. Your highlights should cover: your work, testimonials or reviews, your process, and who you are. A strong profile turns a casual viewer into a warm lead. A weak profile loses them in seconds.

The role of paid reach

Even the best organic content has a ceiling. Instagram's algorithm shows your posts to roughly 3 to 5 percent of your followers. If you want to reach new people — people who don't already know you — you need to amplify your best content with a small paid budget. Even $10 to $20 a day behind your top posts can dramatically increase how many new potential customers see your business.

What to do this week

Update your bio, add a direct link to your contact page, and look back at your last twelve posts. Pick the one that got the most saves or replies and turn it into a paid post for one week. Watch what happens. If you want a clear picture of where your Instagram strategy has gaps, a free visibility audit will show you exactly where to focus.

How to Get Your Sydney Business Found on Google Without Paying for Ads

Local SEO is one of the most underused tools for small businesses — and most of it is completely free.

When someone in your suburb searches for what you do, does your business show up? For most small business owners in Sydney, the honest answer is no — or not reliably. And the frustrating part is that local SEO, done well, is one of the most powerful ways to generate consistent, free, high-intent enquiries. Here's how it works and exactly what to do.

What is local SEO and why does it matter?

Local SEO is the process of optimising your online presence so that Google shows your business to people searching for your services in your area. When someone types "plumber Cronulla" or "graphic designer Sutherland Shire" into Google, the results they see first aren't paid ads — they're the Google Maps listings and organic results that Google has decided are the most relevant. Showing up there is free. But it doesn't happen automatically.

Step 1: Claim and complete your Google Business Profile

If you haven't claimed your Google Business Profile, this is the single highest-impact thing you can do today. Go to business.google.com, claim your listing, and fill out every section completely — your business name, address, phone number, website, hours, categories, and a description that includes what you do and where you're based. Add photos. Real ones, not stock. Businesses with complete profiles get significantly more views and clicks than incomplete ones.

"Your Google Business Profile is your most valuable free piece of real estate on the internet. Most businesses leave it half-empty."

Step 2: Get more Google reviews

Reviews are one of Google's strongest local ranking signals. The more recent, positive reviews you have, the higher you'll appear in local search results. The simplest way to get more reviews is to ask — directly, after every job or positive interaction. Send a follow-up message with a direct link to your Google review page. Make it one tap. Most happy customers will leave a review if you make it easy enough. Aim for a minimum of 20 reviews before you consider your profile competitive.

Step 3: Make sure your website mentions where you work

Google uses your website to understand what you do and where. If your website never mentions "Sydney," "Sutherland Shire," or the specific suburbs you serve, Google won't associate you with those locations. Add your location naturally throughout your website — in your homepage headline, your about page, your service descriptions, and your footer. Don't keyword-stuff. Just write naturally as a business that is proud to serve your local area.

Step 4: Build local citations

A citation is any mention of your business name, address, and phone number on another website. Local directories like True Local, Yellow Pages, Yelp, and industry-specific directories all count. The more consistent and widespread these citations are, the more Google trusts that your business is legitimate and established in your area. Spend an hour making sure your details are accurate and consistent across the main directories.

Step 5: Post regularly on your Google Business Profile

Most businesses don't know that your Google Business Profile has a posts feature — similar to social media. Posting updates, offers, or recent work once or twice a week signals to Google that your business is active. It also gives potential customers more reasons to choose you when they find your listing. It takes five minutes and almost nobody does it, which means it's a genuine competitive advantage.

How long does local SEO take?

Local SEO is not instant. Expect to see meaningful movement in your rankings within two to four months of consistent effort. But unlike paid ads, the results compound — a strong local SEO foundation keeps working for you long after you've built it. If you want to know where your biggest local SEO gaps are right now, a free visibility audit will show you exactly where to focus first.

The Real Difference Between Organic Reach and Paid Advertising

Two different tools. Two different jobs. Here's how to know which one you need — and when.

One of the most common questions we get from Sydney business owners is this: should I be running ads, or should I focus on my organic content? The honest answer is both — but they do very different things, and understanding the difference changes how you invest your marketing budget.

What is organic reach?

Organic reach is any visibility you earn without paying for it. That includes your Instagram posts, your Google Business Profile, your website showing up in search results, and word of mouth. Organic is powerful because it builds trust over time. When someone finds you through a Google search or sees your content consistently, they arrive already knowing something about you. That makes them much easier to convert into a customer.

What are paid ads?

Paid advertising is exactly that — you pay to put your business in front of people. Google Ads show your business at the top of search results when someone types in a relevant keyword. Meta Ads (Facebook and Instagram) show your business to people based on their location, interests, and behaviour. Paid ads are powerful because they are immediate and scalable. You can turn them on and start getting visibility within hours.

"Organic reaches the people already looking for you. Paid ads reach the people who don't know you exist yet."

The problem with relying only on organic

Organic content takes time to build momentum. The Instagram algorithm shows your posts to a small percentage of your followers — typically 3 to 5 percent. Without paid reach behind your content, you're essentially posting to a small room. Organic is essential, but if you want to grow beyond your existing audience, it's not enough on its own.

The problem with relying only on paid ads

Paid ads without a strong organic foundation is like pouring water into a leaking bucket. If someone clicks your ad and lands on a website that looks outdated, or finds your Instagram with three posts from six months ago — they won't convert. Paid ads drive traffic. Your organic presence converts it.

The system that works

The businesses generating consistent enquiries are doing both. A professional website that converts. Consistent social content that builds trust. Paid ads that drive volume. Website is the foundation. Content is the trust builder. Paid ads are the accelerator. In that order.

So which should you start with?

If you don't have a strong website and consistent content yet — start there. Paid ads on a weak foundation is wasted budget. Once your foundation is solid, add ads and watch what happens to your enquiry volume.

5 Signs Your Website Is Losing You Leads Every Day

A website that doesn't convert isn't an asset. It's a liability.

Most business owners assume that having a website means they're covered online. But having a website and having a website that generates enquiries are two very different things. Here are five signs your website is quietly costing you leads every single day.

1. It takes more than 3 seconds to load

If your website takes longer than three seconds to load, more than half your visitors will leave before they even see it. Page speed is one of the most overlooked factors in both SEO ranking and conversion. A slow website signals to Google that your site isn't worth showing — and signals to visitors that your business isn't worth their time. Test your site speed at pagespeed.web.dev and aim for a score above 80 on mobile.

2. There's no clear call to action above the fold

"Above the fold" means what someone sees before they scroll. If your website's top section doesn't tell someone exactly what you do and what they should do next — you're losing leads. Every page should have one clear, prominent call to action: Call now, Book a free consultation, Get a quote. If a visitor has to search for how to contact you, most of them won't bother.

3. It doesn't look right on mobile

More than 60 percent of website traffic comes from mobile devices. If your website isn't designed for mobile — text that's too small, buttons too close together, images that don't scale — visitors will leave immediately. Pull out your phone right now and load your website. If anything feels awkward or hard to use, that's a conversion problem.

"Your website is your hardest-working salesperson. If it's slow, confusing, or embarrassing — it's losing you jobs every day."

4. You can't easily update it

A website you can't update is a website that goes stale. If adding a new service, updating your pricing, or posting a photo requires calling a developer — you're going to stop updating it. And a website that hasn't been touched in two years signals to visitors and to Google that your business may not be active. Your website should have a simple CMS so you can make basic updates yourself.

5. You're embarrassed to share the link

This is the simplest test of all. If a potential client asked for your website right now, would you feel confident sending it? Or would you add a disclaimer like "it's a bit outdated"? If you're hesitant to share your own website, that's the clearest sign it's not working for you. Your website should be something you're proud to share — because it represents the quality of your work.

What to do next

If any of these five signs sound familiar, the good news is that they're all fixable. The question is where to start. A free visibility audit will show you exactly which issues are costing you the most enquiries — and what to prioritise first.

Word of Mouth Is a Strategy — Just Not One You Can Scale

Referrals are great. But if your only pipeline is people telling their friends, you don't have a marketing strategy. You have a ceiling.

Word of mouth is how most small businesses in Sydney get their first clients. And for a while, it works well. You do good work, people tell their friends, the phone keeps ringing. But at some point — usually when you want to grow, or when a few key referrers go quiet — the cracks appear. The pipeline dries up and you realise you have no control over where the next job is coming from.

Why word of mouth feels like a strategy

Referrals feel like a strategy because they produce real results. Happy clients, warm leads, easy conversions. The quality of referral enquiries is almost always higher than cold traffic — the person arrives already trusting you. So it's easy to look at a full calendar and assume the marketing is working. But what you're actually running is a passive system that depends entirely on other people's actions and timing. You can't turn it up when you need more work. You can't aim it at a new market. And you can't rely on it to stay consistent.

"Referrals are a result of doing great work. Marketing is the system that makes sure new people find you in the first place."

The problem with a referral-only business

There are four specific ways that relying only on word of mouth limits your business. First, your growth is capped by the size of your existing network — you can only grow as fast as your current clients refer you. Second, you have no control over the type of work you attract — referrals tend to send you more of what you already do, making it hard to move into new markets or service areas. Third, your revenue becomes unpredictable — feast and famine cycles are a hallmark of businesses without an active marketing system. Fourth, your business is fragile — lose a few key clients or referrers and your pipeline can disappear overnight.

What a real marketing system looks like

A marketing system is anything that reliably and repeatedly puts your business in front of new potential customers. That includes your website showing up in Google search. Your Google Business Profile appearing in local map results. Your social media content reaching people who don't already know you. Paid advertising targeting your ideal customer by location and interest. Each of these channels works independently and compounds over time. Together, they create a pipeline you control — rather than one that controls you.

Word of mouth still matters — but it works better with a system behind it

None of this means referrals aren't valuable. They are. But when someone is referred to you, the first thing they do is Google you. They check your website, look at your reviews, scroll your Instagram. If what they find doesn't match the recommendation, you lose the lead. A strong digital presence makes your word of mouth more effective — it validates the referral and makes it easier for the prospect to say yes.

How to start building a system

You don't need to overhaul everything at once. Start with the two highest-impact foundations: a website that clearly communicates what you do and how to contact you, and a complete, review-rich Google Business Profile. From there, add consistent social content and, when you're ready, a small paid advertising budget. Build one layer at a time. If you want to know where your current gaps are and what to prioritise first, a free visibility audit is the best place to start.
